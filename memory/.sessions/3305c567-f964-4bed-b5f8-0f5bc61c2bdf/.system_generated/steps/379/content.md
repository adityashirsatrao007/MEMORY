<section id="what-is-scrollytelling">
<h2>What is scrollytelling?</h2>

<p><strong>Scrollytelling</strong> (a blend of <strong>scroll</strong> and <strong>storytelling</strong>) is a web design technique where the narrative unfolds as the user scrolls the website. The first examples of scrollytelling started appearing on the web over a decade ago, with well-known pieces like The New York Times' <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/projects/2012/snow-fall/index.html#/?part=tunnel-creek" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Snow Fall: The Avalanche at Tunnel Creek</a>.</p>

<p>Scrollytelling helps tell a story in an immersive way, instead of writing text enriched with images and maybe video embeds. It feels like the reader "controls" the page and is part of the story, helping grab and keep attention when <a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01449290500330448" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">you have 50 milliseconds to make a good first impression</a>.</p>

<p>There are 3 main key things about scrollytelling:</p>

<ul class="key-list">
  <li><strong>Pacing ("Remote Control")</strong> - The reader is in control of how they read. If they scroll fast, the story moves fast; if they stop, the story freezes. This gives total control over complex information.</li>
  <li><strong>Triggered Animation</strong> - Unlike traditional websites where things just render, in scrollytelling, elements "activate" when they reach a certain point on the screen. A chart might draw itself, a background might transform from a forest to a city, or a video starts playing automatically when it reaches the center of the page.</li>
  <li><strong>The "Reveal" Mechanic</strong> - Information is served in bite-sized chunks. It prevents content fatigue (when the reader feels overwhelmed by a wall of text) by only showing what the reader needs at that exact moment.</li>
</ul>

<p>You can read more about what scrollytelling is in our article <a href="https://scrollytelling.ai/about/">about scrollytelling</a>, explore our <a href="https://scrollytelling.ai/scrollytelling-tools/">complete guide to the best scrollytelling tools and digital storytelling platforms</a>, or study curated <a href="https://scrollytelling.ai/feature-writing-examples/">feature writing examples</a> to see how professional journalists structure compelling narratives.</p>
</section>

<h2>27 scrollytelling examples to inspire your content</h2>

<p>Scroll-based visual storytelling, packed with interactive elements, significantly boosts visitor engagement. When executed correctly, these digital stories perform exceptionally well from an SEO perspective.</p>

<p>Key SEO considerations for scrollytelling:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Dwell Time</strong>: Scrollytelling websites typically see higher dwell times and longer average engagement. This naturally reduces bounce rates - a major signal that tells Google and other search engines to rank a website higher.</li>
  <li><strong>Speed and Responsiveness</strong>: Page load speed is one of the critical SEO factors. Because scrollytelling content is often media-heavy, professional optimization is essential. Without it, performance and SEO rankings will suffer.</li>
</ul>

<p>Therefore, as we review these standout scrollytelling examples, we will focus not only on the visual and interactive experience but also on their technical execution.</p>

<section id="benefits">
<h2>The benefits of scrollytelling in web design</h2>

<p>Scrollytelling transforms a passive "wall of text" into an active, <strong>cinematic journey</strong> where the user controls the tempo. By tethering animations and data reveals directly to the physical act of scrolling, you create a <strong>reward-based feedback loop</strong> - every flick of the finger provides a visual payoff that keeps the reader moving.</p>

<p><strong>Why it wins:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Cognitive Ease:</strong> Complex data is "unveiled" in bite-sized, sequential layers, preventing information overload.</li>
  <li><strong>Bespoke Pacing:</strong> The user becomes the director, lingering on details or breezing through the narrative at their own speed.</li>
  <li><strong>Emotional Hook:</strong> It bridges the gap between static reading and video, providing the immersion of a film with the interactivity of a website.</li>
</ul>

<p>Essentially, scrollytelling doesn't just present information; it <strong>engineers an experience</strong> that significantly increases dwell time and message retention. These same principles - pacing, emotional hooks, and strategic information reveals - are also core to <a href="https://scrollytelling.ai/feature-writing-examples/">feature writing</a>.</p>
</section>

<!-- ==================== WHEN TO USE ==================== -->
<section id="when-to-use">
<h2>When to use scrollytelling</h2>

<p>Scrollytelling is a high-stakes design choice. The rule is simple - use it to <strong>narrate</strong>, not just to <strong>decorate</strong>.</p>

<div class="ua-grid">
  <div class="ua-card do">
    <h4>Use it when:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>The Story is Linear:</strong> Your content has a clear beginning, middle, and end (e.g., a "Year in Review" or a product's origin story).</li>
      <li><strong>The Product is Complex:</strong> You need to "unfold" technical layers, like an exploded 3D view of a watch or a step-by-step software tour.</li>
      <li><strong>Data needs Pacing:</strong> You have heavy statistics that would overwhelm a reader if shown all at once. Scrolling "unveils" data points one by one.</li>
      <li><strong>You're Selling a "Vibe":</strong> For luxury or fashion, scrollytelling creates an immersive, cinematic atmosphere that static images can't match.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="ua-card dont">
    <h4>Avoid it when:</h4>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Speed is the Priority:</strong> If a user is looking for a "Contact Us" page or a support document, scrollytelling is just a barrier.</li>
      <li><strong>Mobile Performance is Weak:</strong> If you can't optimize for a Performance Score of 90+, don't force a 15-second loading screen on a mobile user.</li>
      <li><strong>Content is Frequent:</strong> It's too expensive and time-consuming for daily blog posts or news cycles.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<section id="faq" class="faq">
<h2>FAQ</h2>
<p>The most common questions answered with a focus on performance, narrative, and technical reality.</p>

<details class="faq-item">
  <summary>How does scrollytelling work?</summary>
  <div class="fa">
    <p>Technically, a script (like <a href="https://gsap.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GSAP</a> or a native Intersection Observer) tracks the user's vertical or horizontal scroll progress. As the user moves, the script "scrubs" through an animation timeline - whether that's moving a 3D model, changing text opacity, or playing a video frame-by-frame.</p>
    <blockquote><p><strong>The Key:</strong> The user becomes the "conductor" - when they stop scrolling, the story stops. If they scroll fast, the story accelerates. This creates a physical link between the reader and the content.</p></blockquote>
  </div>
</details>

<details class="faq-item">
  <summary>Is scrollytelling right for you?</summary>
  <div class="fa">
    <p>It depends on your goal. Ask yourself these 3 questions:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Is my message complex?</strong> If you're explaining a 10-step manufacturing process or a 50-page annual report - <strong>Yes</strong>.</li>
      <li><strong>Is my brand "Premium"?</strong> If you need to establish authority and "WOW" factor - <strong>Yes</strong>.</li>
      <li><strong>Is my user looking for a quick answer?</strong> If they just want a price list or a support phone number - <strong>No</strong>. Scrollytelling is designed mostly for <em>exploring</em>, not <em>searching</em>.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</details>

<details class="faq-item">
  <summary>What makes a good scrollytelling story?</summary>
  <div class="fa">
    <p>A great scrollytelling piece follows:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Pacing -</strong> Don't overwhelm the user. Provide "breathing room" between major visual transitions.</li>
      <li><strong>Purpose -</strong> Every animation should serve the data. If a 3D model spins just because it looks cool, it's a distraction, not storytelling.</li>
      <li><strong>Performance -</strong> A 10-second load time is not the way to go. A good story must be <strong>lean with good performance</strong>. As seen in our reviews above, an Adidas-level 94 score is the gold standard.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</details>

<details class="faq-item">
  <summary>What's the difference between scrollytelling and parallax?</summary>
  <div class="fa">
    <p>While often confused, they serve different use cases:</p>
    <table class="cmp">
      <thead>
        <tr><th>Feature</th><th>Parallax Scrolling</th><th>Scrollytelling</th></tr>
      </thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td><strong>Primary Goal</strong></td><td>Visual depth / aesthetic</td><td>Narrative progression</td></tr>
        <tr><td><strong>Trigger</strong></td><td>Layers moving at different speeds</td><td>Content changes triggered by scroll depth</td></tr>
        <tr><td><strong>Interaction</strong></td><td>Passive (looks cool as you pass)</td><td>Active (you "unlock" the next part)</td></tr>
        <tr><td><strong>Best For</strong></td><td>Landing pages &amp; backgrounds</td><td>Reports, tutorials, and deep-dives</td></tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>
  </div>
</details>

<details class="faq-item">
  <summary>Is scrollytelling good for SEO?</summary>
  <div class="fa">
    <p>This is a double-edged sword:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>It increases <strong>Dwell Time</strong> (the time people stay on your page) - a huge positive signal for Google. Some experts say this is the most important factor.</li>
      <li>If not built correctly, scrollytelling sites can appear "empty" to search crawlers because the content is hidden behind JavaScript, or too heavy because of many images, videos, and other media.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</details>
